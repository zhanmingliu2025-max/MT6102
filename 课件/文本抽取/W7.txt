[p22] 3.1 Polluter-Pays Principle (PPP) | • Commonly accepted practice that those who produce  | pollution should bear the costs of managing it to  | prevent damage to human health or the environment. | • GHG considered a form of pollution because they  | cause potential harm and damage through impacts on  | the climate.
[p23] 3.1 Polluter-Pays Principle (PPP) | • When pollution cost from the release of GHG not  | imposed on emitters (i.e. ships), these costs are  | ‘externalised’ to society. | • PPP can be applied to GHG through a so-called ‘______  | _________’.  |   | > imposes a charge on the emission of GHG  | equivalent to the corresponding potential cost caused  | through future climate change = force emitters to  | internalise cost of pollution.
[p24] 3.1 Polluter-Pays Principle (PPP) | • Two different policy instruments: | (1) __________ – price of pollution determined by rate of tax  | for each tonne of GHG emitted. | (2) _____________________ (ETS) – sets a cap/limit on max  | level of emissions for a given time period and distributes  | permits or allowances for each unit of GHG among firms  | that produce emissions.
[p25] 3.2 What is EU ETS? | • An emissions cap-and-trade system that aims to  | reduce GHG emissions by setting a limit on GHG  | emissions. | • Each year, a limited amount of ____________ (EUAs) is  | made available for trading in the market.  |   | - reduced yearly to meet its target of 55% reduction  | by 2030 relative to 1990 and net zero by 2050. |   | - each EUA gives companies a right to emit one tonne  | of C02 equivalent.
[p26] 3.2.1 What are EUAs?  | • A type of carbon allowance that allows companies covered  | by the EU ETS to emit a certain amount of CO2. | • Can be bought and sold on the market. | • Variable market price of EUAs reflects cost of reducing  | emissions.

[p79] 5.3.2 Bond Issue | • Traditionally financing ships with bonds issuance not prime  | choice for shipowners: |   | - shipping has credit ratings that are below investment  | grade; |   | - high public debt default rate; |   | - bonds issued fall into the high-yield bond category with  | interest rate on the high side. | • Development of bond market and risk perception by the  | investors > lack of confidence will negatively affect the use  | of bonds or at high interest rate and small amount.
[p80] 5.3.2 Bond Issue | Advantages | • Provide creditworthy private companies which do not wish  | to go down the public equity route. | • Used by established public shipping companies with  | significant market capitalisation, credit status, and  | relationship with investment institutions to raise relatively  | large amounts of capital quickly, easily and flexibly.  | • Compared with bank debt, bonds offer longer term of  | financing, typically 10-15 years.
[p81] 5.3.2 Bond Issue | Disadvantages | • Coupon requirements will be demanding due to the speculative  | ratings assigned by rating agencies. | • Bond holders’ tolerance for default of interest payment is low: | – dangers of default would then legally entitle the investor to  | petition for winding up of the business as it is a debt in  | nature and normally does not attach with any collateral.
